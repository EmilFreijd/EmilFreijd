Standard lists and notes rarely help when the real problem is the gap between intention, behaviour, and honest follow-through. It's not that the next step is unclear — it's that there's nothing making it harder to drift. The usual tools archive your intentions without holding you to them.

The accountability agent changes that relationship. Instead of functioning as a passive archive, it gathers focus, asks better questions, and surfaces the next step before you drift. It holds tasks, current focus, and recurring reflection prompts. Each session creates a clear next step and reminds you why that step matters — not just what it is. It feels more like a coach than a to-do list.

What makes it useful isn't the automation. It's that the agent makes it structurally harder to ignore what you said you wanted to work on. The friction between intention and action shrinks because there's something actively tracking the gap.

Built from a real problem, with real usage as the measure of success. The focus throughout has been on what actually changes behaviour — not what looks impressive in a demo. That distinction matters more than most people admit when building tools for themselves.
