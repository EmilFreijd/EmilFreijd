In the [Design Thinking Lab at the Swedish National Courts Administration](/stream/design-thinking-lab/), we generated and grouped ideas before prioritising them. The matrix helped the group choose an ambitious concept they could prototype. We built a prototype to give coordinators a shared view of staffing and schedules. The example ideas below are fictional and deal with a similar problem.

<IdeaMatrix lang="en" />

## How I use the matrix

1. Start with a problem the group agrees on and put one idea on each card. Agree on the time, resources and authority you have when judging feasibility.
2. Sort the ideas together. **Wow** combines originality and feasibility. **Now** is familiar and easier to implement. **How** contains original ideas that need more work or better conditions. **Ciao** is familiar and hard to implement within the current constraints.
3. Pick one or two ideas to test. Write down what you want to learn, how you could test it and who will take the next step. Copy the summary into the workshop notes.

The matrix makes it easier to discuss why an idea lands where it does. The assessment reflects the group's assumptions and can change as you learn more. Before choosing a concept to develop, ask how it would help the people experiencing the problem.
